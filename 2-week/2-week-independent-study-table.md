# Week 2 – Independent Study Timetable

After Monday 2, plan approximately **28 hours of independent study** before the next contact session.

The schedule below is a suggested way to keep the work within normal weekday hours. You may adjust the timing, but complete the required work before Monday.

| Day           | Suggested time | Main work                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------- | -------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Tuesday**   |       **~7 h** | **Study classification theory** – features and target, binary and multiclass classification, training and test data, baseline models, predictions, confusion matrix, accuracy, precision, recall and data leakage. Study the basic scikit-learn workflow and complete the introductory questions. |
| **Wednesday** |       **~7 h** | **Classification practical** – understand the provided classification problem, define `X` and `y`, inspect class distributions, split the data, create a baseline, and train at least two classifiers.                                                                                            |
| **Thursday**  |       **~7 h** | **Evaluation and preprocessing** – evaluate classifiers using confusion matrices and suitable metrics, compare against the baseline, investigate preprocessing and data leakage, and use a pipeline where appropriate.                                                                            |
| **Friday**    |       **~7 h** | **Complete and connect to the project** – finish the practical exercise, consider whether classification is suitable for your project, update the Project Data Plan, prepare your concept map, complete the theory quiz and practical verification task, and review difficult concepts.           |

## Main study sources

* **Course material:** *Classification: From a Question to a Valid Prediction*
* **Python Data Science Handbook:** *Introducing Scikit-Learn* – features, target, estimator API, `fit()` and `predict()` workflow. ([Pythonic Perambulations][1])
* **Zaki & Meira:** selected material from Chapter 18 *Probabilistic Classification* and Chapter 19 *Decision Tree Classifier*. The classification part of the book also contains a separate assessment chapter. ([Data Mining and Machine Learning][2])
* **scikit-learn:** *Common pitfalls and recommended practices* – especially preprocessing and data leakage. ([Scikit-learn][3])
* **scikit-learn:** confusion matrix and classification evaluation. ([Scikit-learn][4])
* **Optional practical support:** ISLP Classification Lab with logistic regression, LDA/QDA, Naive Bayes and KNN. ([Intro Stat Learning][5])

## Before next Monday

You should have:

* studied the assigned classification theory;
* completed the individual classification practical;
* compared your classifiers with a baseline;
* evaluated predictions using suitable metrics;
* considered possible data leakage;
* prepared your individual **concept map**;
* updated your group's **Project Data Plan**;
* completed the theory quiz and practical verification task.

The goal is to arrive on Monday already able to build and evaluate a basic classification workflow. Monday can then be used for **deeper classification work, problem solving, discussion and project support**.
