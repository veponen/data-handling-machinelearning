# Assessment and Weekly Work

Students study introductory material before each Monday session. Monday contact teaching is used mainly for explanations, demonstrations, guided exercises, problem solving, discussion, and project work.

A typical week consists of:

* preparation material, a concept map, and a short automatically graded quiz before Monday;
* a 6-hour Monday teaching and workshop session;
* individual practical exercises during the week;
* project work and preparation for the next topic.

## Assessment

* **Preparation quizzes and concept maps – 10%**
* **Practical exercises and automated verification – 20%**
* **Practical data processing and machine learning group project – 30%**
* **Individual demonstration of project understanding – 10%**
* **Final individual exam – 30%**

## Practical exercises

Practical exercises are normally **individual and mandatory**.

Each student submits their own work. Exercises are assessed mainly as:

**PASS / REVISE / MISSING**

Not every submission is necessarily inspected in detail. The teacher may check any submission and may ask the student to explain or demonstrate their solution. Work marked for revision must be corrected.

On the following Monday, group members compare their individual solutions, discuss important findings and difficulties, and may be asked to present selected results to the class.

The practical-exercise grade is based mainly on completion and demonstrated understanding of the required exercises. Some exercises may also include automated verification.

## Project and final exam

The final exam uses mainly automatically graded questions and assesses understanding of the main concepts, methods, and their appropriate use.

The group project assesses the ability to apply data handling and machine learning methods to a practical problem, including data preparation, method selection, validation, visualization, and interpretation.

Both **individual understanding and practical application** are important. Students are expected not only to use machine learning tools, but also to understand why particular methods and preprocessing choices are appropriate for the data and problem being studied.
