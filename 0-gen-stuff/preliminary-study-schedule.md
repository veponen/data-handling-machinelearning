# Preliminary Study Schedule

**Data Processing and Machine Learning – 10 ECTS**

This is an **intensive full-time course**, not a course consisting only of the Monday contact session.

At Metropolia, **1 ECTS corresponds to approximately 27 hours of student work**. Therefore, a 10 ECTS course represents approximately **270 hours of work**.

The course runs for eight weeks:

* **Total workload:** approximately 270 hours
* **Average workload:** approximately 34 hours/week
* **Contact teaching:** Monday 09:00–16:00, lunch 12:00–13:00 = 6 h/week
* **Contact teaching in total:** 48 hours
* **Independent study:** approximately 222 hours = 28 h/week

The workload is designed to fit mainly into **normal weekday daytime study hours**. If you follow the weekly schedule, evenings and weekends should normally remain available. Individual students may, however, need different amounts of time.

## Weekly working rhythm

From Week 2 onward, students should prepare for each Monday session in advance.

**Before Monday**

* study the assigned core theory;
* prepare a **simplified concept map** containing concepts you consider important, interesting, difficult, or strongly connected;
* complete a short preparation quiz when assigned.

**Monday 09:00–16:00**

Monday sessions are used mainly for:

* clarification and discussion of the theory studied beforehand;
* demonstrations;
* guided practical exercises;
* problem solving;
* group project work and project support.

**During the rest of the week**

* practical data-processing and machine-learning exercises;
* project work;
* study of the next topic;
* preparation of the next concept map.

## Group project

Students work in small groups on a **predefined practical challenge**. Several challenges may belong to the same application area but address different problems.

The project follows the general workflow:

**understand the problem → understand the data → prepare the data → select methods → model → validate → visualize → interpret**

Synthetic or artificial test-data generation may also form part of the project.

## Planned assessment

* Preparation quizzes – **10%**
* Practical exercises and verification – **20%**
* Practical data processing and machine learning group project – **30%**
* Individual demonstration of project understanding – **10%**
* Final individual exam – **30%**

## Study material

The main online textbook is:

**Jake VanderPlas: *Python Data Science Handbook***
The complete book and its Jupyter notebooks are available online. It will be used especially for Python data handling, NumPy, pandas, visualization, preprocessing and introductory machine learning.

Selected supplementary material will be used from:

* **Mohammed J. Zaki & Wagner Meira Jr.: *Data Mining and Machine Learning: Fundamental Concepts and Algorithms*** – particularly association analysis, clustering, classification and regression.
* **James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani & Taylor: *An Introduction to Statistical Learning with Applications in Python*** – particularly statistical learning, model evaluation, classification and regression.
* **Daniel Jurafsky & James H. Martin: *Speech and Language Processing*** – selected material for natural language processing.

Additional course material will be provided where these sources do not give a sufficiently direct introduction to the required topic, for example **data types, their meaning, valid operations, preprocessing choices, and project-specific instructions**.
